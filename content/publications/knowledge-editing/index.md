abstract: |
  Knowledge Editing (KE) aims to alter specific facts stored in the model, but we show that current evaluations overstate its practical reliability through reliance on short-output matching. Using a free-form generation protocol across realistic question types and multi-turn dialogue, we evaluate five representative KE methods across two models and find that nominal edit success often masks substantially lower usable generation quality. We then propose a failure mode taxonomy showing that many nominally successful edits are degraded by repetition, contradictory mixtures, cognitive dissonance hallucinations, and locality errors. These findings suggest that KE methods should move beyond binary success toward generative stability.
